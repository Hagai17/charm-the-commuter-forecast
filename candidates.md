# Candidates — Charm the Commuter: Forecast a City That Isn't

Gate: one line, 1–140 characters, written to /output/entry.txt.
Judge (bored commuter at a rainy bus stop) weighs, in order:
1. Reads as a one-line weather forecast for a city that does not exist — city named, weather the whole point.
2. Makes a bored commuter smile on one reading — small joke, surprising image, turn of phrase.
3. Keeps the register of a real forecast — broadcast rhythm bent rather than dropped.
4. One concrete detail of the city's weather or its life, so it feels like a place.
5. Every word earns its place. Quirkiness for its own sake does not count.

Playbook applied: concrete commuter-true detail over whimsy; the joke must live
inside the forecast grammar itself (bend a real forecast phrase), strongest beat last.

## Drafts

1. Puddlewick: rain until noon, clearing to drizzle by three; the ducks have been notified and are, frankly, delighted.
2. Greater Umbraville: heavy showers all morning, easing by lunch; highs of 14, lows of everyone's umbrella, left on the 8:15 again.
3. New Soggington: persistent rain, turning to soup by evening; visibility poor, spirits damp, the 42 bus still somehow late.
4. Drizzlemere: light rain, 12 degrees, winds from the northwest; the market square floods at high tide of umbrellas around five.
5. Fort Precipitation: scattered showers with a 90% chance of the mayor finally fixing that one leaking bus shelter roof.
6. Lake Moisture: overcast with drizzle on and off all day; the ferry runs, the gulls complain, nobody's hair survives the crossing.
7. Port Regret: morning fog lifting to reveal more fog; highs of 11, with outbreaks of sighing at the station from six.
8. Upper Dampford: rain at first light, easing mid-morning; the river is up, the ducks are up earlier, and both are showing off.
9. Saint Galoshes: wet, 13 degrees, gusting; a day for the good coat — the one you keep meaning to reproof since last March.
10. Mizzleburgh: fine rain that doesn't count as rain until you're soaked; trains run, tempers soak faster, home by six, just.

## Duel notes (judge criteria, in order)

- 1 vs 8: both lean on ducks. 1's "notified and delighted" is whimsy-forward — quirk
  for its own sake, which the brief explicitly discounts. 8 is tighter but still duck-led. Both fall early.
- 3 vs 6: 3's "turning to soup" is a fun bend but the 42-bus gag is generic; 6's
  "nobody's hair survives the crossing" is a concrete commuter image. 6 advances.
- 4 vs 9: 4's "high tide of umbrellas" is a pretty image but decorative; 9's
  unreproofed good coat is a real commuter detail. 9 advances, narrowly.
- 5 vs 7: 5's leaking bus-shelter roof is the most civic-concrete detail in the set,
  but the line reads as a complaint more than a forecast. 7 keeps pure broadcast
  register ("highs of 11, with outbreaks of sighing") and bends it once. 7 advances.
- 10 vs 2: 10's mizzle definition is charming but explains its own joke. 2 bends the
  highs/lows formula itself — "lows of everyone's umbrella, left on the 8:15 again" —
  a loss every commuter has lived, delivered in forecast grammar. 2 advances clearly.
- Semifinal 6 vs 7: 6 is warm; 7 is drier and more broadcast-true. Criterion 3
  (register bent, not dropped) decides it: 7.
- Semifinal 9 vs 2: 9's coat is relatable, but the joke sits outside the forecast
  numbers. 2's joke IS a forecast number (the "lows"). Criterion 2 + 3: 2.
- Final 7 vs 2: both keep register and land one bend. 7's "outbreaks of sighing" is
  atmosphere; 2's lost umbrella on the 8:15 is a specific, visual, commuter-true
  detail (criterion 4) with the smile arriving on the last phrase (criterion 2).
  Reversed order check: reading 2 first then 7, 7 feels thinner — sighing is a mood,
  the umbrella is a story. Winner confirmed both ways.

## Winner

Greater Umbraville: heavy showers all morning, easing by lunch; highs of 14, lows of everyone's umbrella, left on the 8:15 again.
